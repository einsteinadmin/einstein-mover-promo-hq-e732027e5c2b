Horstman (Response A) Manager Tools
The scoreboard will crowd out feedback if you let it. A score documents feedback; it never delivers it. This design has that trap, and monthly re-qualification makes it worse. A BM who's underwater — new huddle cadence, hiring, Training Days, 81% turnover — will do the monthly review INSTEAD of feedback, not in addition to it. The system becomes the manager. "Check the matrix" replaces "when you left the dolly strap loose, here's what happened." That's role power substituting for relationship power, and relationship power is the only thing that retains movers you've already paid to season. No mover should ever be surprised by their status — that's the test. If a mover learns on the 1st that his clock reset to zero and hears it from a spreadsheet, not his manager, you've built a demotivation engine with good intentions. The one-month full reset is indefensible from a manager's chair: one bad month — a damage claim not fully his fault, a sick kid wrecking attendance — and he's back at zero? Managers will either stop delivering that news honestly or start gaming the inputs. Try three of the last four months at bar, or reset to month two, not zero. On the CEO's framing: fairness through automaticity is right, but it doesn't remove subjectivity — it RELOCATES it. Damages get contested, attendance gets coded, evals get signed. Whoever adjudicates those edge cases holds the real promotion power, invisibly; name that adjudication process now or fairness dies in the appeals. "Great metrics, bad attitude" is an adjusting-feedback problem: the CHOICES pass/fail only works if managers have documented, delivered redirects before the gate fires — wire it to the Coaching Protocol; no CHOICES fail without a feedback trail. And do not ship this as a manual monthly BM task — you'll get pencil-whipped reviews by month three. STRONGEST REC: every promotion-status change — up, down, or clock-reset — is delivered verbally by the manager in a scheduled one-on-one before it takes effect; the conversation, not the matrix, is the management.

Call 1 — Streak engine (hours-weighted average)

Cameron's call: measure promotion streaks on an hours-weighted rolling average with a minimum floor — not calendar months, which punish part-timers and slow seasons.

Where it landed: all four voices accept the math as fairer — but it only ships if a mover can check his own status in the app in ~30 seconds (his score, the bar, his hours building). If that gauge can't be built, fall back to the simpler rule: "3 of the last 4 months at bar, none below the floor."

Unpack the detail

Hours-weighted means a 40-hour month can't count the same as a 160-hour month — the average runs over hours worked (roughly the last ~500), not over months. The floor is a minimum no single stretch can drop below, so one bad run can't hide inside a good average. The "30-second cab test" is the council's shipping condition: if a mover sitting in the truck can't understand where he stands in 30 seconds, the formula is too clever to publish — hence the app gauge requirement. Weaver added a refinement for consideration: check the floor across the full window, but let eligibility update on the most recent ~250 hours, so a mover who's genuinely improved gets rewarded quickly instead of dragging old months behind him.

Call 4 — Sticky rungs

Where it landed: passes. The house voice called it the same philosophy we already applied in May's comp cycle (Hall-of-Famers kept their earned rates). Unpack the detail

"HOF Path B applied forward" (Advocate): in the May mover comp cycle, the 10 Hall of Fame movers kept their earned higher rates as a protected carve-out rather than being cut to the new ladder — sticky rungs is that same "earned pay doesn't go backward" principle made permanent. Red-circle freeze (Weaver) is the standard comp term: when someone's rate sits above where their performance bar says it should, you freeze the rate (no further increases) rather than cut it — the ask is to define the trigger conditions NOW, not improvise later. Damages attribution — single vs. multi-mover moves

The question: most moves run multiple movers on one crew. When a damage happens and it's not obvious whose fault it was, how does the damages score get split among the movers who were on that job?

  1. Build the technical capability for individual-fault attribution now.

Mechanics, not history

How It Works

What this actually means for you, whether you manage the process or you're the one being evaluated. Numbers that are still being finalized are marked TBD — check the Framework tab for the latest.

Branch Managers

For Branch Managers

The monthly rhythm

Around the 1st of each month, the system checks who's crossed an hours mark and flags them for review — split into qualified (hours and every performance metric clear the bar) and not qualified (tells you exactly which metric is holding them back).

Your job

Approve a qualified promotion, or deny it. Approving it in the tool does not automatically push it into Rippling — at least not yet. You'll still need to go into Rippling and enter the promotion yourself once it's approved.

Hours alone don't get anyone promoted

A mover who hits the hours required to be considered but doesn't clear the required floor on the performance metrics will not be promoted — no exceptions for tenure. This is part of Einstein's continued move from an entitlement-based culture to a performance-based one, and the standard is the same for everyone. The bar applies to each metric on its own — attendance, driver score, and damages each have to individually clear their bar; it's never a blended average across the three. (Each metric's own number is itself an average over its measurement window — that averaging happens within a metric, never across metrics.)

What counts

  • Attendance — lates and call-outs
  • Driver score (Samsara) — their own driving: no rolling stops, no phone use, safe following distance
  • Damages — scored for the whole crew on a job, not pinned on one person unless someone's clearly and individually responsible

Exception: Lead in Training (LIT) is scored on Attendance and Damages only — no driver score, since LITs aren't driving the truck yet.

Lead in Training → Part-Time Lead also needs your judgment

This gate is business-need-gated too, not just a hard number. A mover clears LIT's own hours and bar (455 hrs / 7.0), but actually moving into a Part-Time Lead seat depends on whether there's a business need for another Lead. Hours + performance get someone eligible; the seat opening is your call.

Part-Time Lead → Junior Lead needs your judgment, not just data

A separate, later gate. A Part-Time Lead becomes eligible at 400 hours worked as a lead, or 80%+ of jobs led across 2 back-to-back pay periods — but promotion is based on business need for a Lead. If there's no need right now, that's a real "not yet," not a system error. Note: hours worked as a Part-Time Lead don't carry forward into the Junior Lead hours count toward Senior Lead — that clock starts fresh at Junior Lead.

What to tell someone who has the hours but not the bar

Be specific: "you've got the hours, but your Samsara score needs to come up before you clear the bar" — not a vague "not this time." They're automatically re-evaluated next month; there's nothing for them to file or request.

When a promotion actually takes effect

Any promotion goes live at the start of the following pay period — never mid-period. That keeps the title change and the pay change landing on the same day, so there's no gap where someone's doing the new job at the old rate. Note: pay periods currently start on Saturdays, so in practice you'll generally be making a promotion effective on a Saturday.

No grandfathering

Everyone gets evaluated under the new rules the moment this launches — including someone who was about to hit an old tenure-only promotion. Nobody loses their current title or pay; this only changes what earns the next one.

Movers

For Movers

Two ways to get promoted

Standard track: work enough hours in your current role, and keep your attendance, driving, and damages all above the minimum bar. Once both are true, you're eligible. Each of the three has to individually clear its own bar — it's never a blended average across all three.

Fast track: if your performance is excellent on all three — well above the standard bar — over roughly your last 4 months, you can get promoted with fewer hours worked. The bar for "excellent" is 9 for Junior Lead and Regular Lead, and 10 for Senior Lead and Master Lead.

What counts

  • Attendance — lates and call-outs
  • Driver score (Samsara) — your own driving: no rolling stops, no phone use, safe following distance
  • Damages — scored for the whole crew on a job, not pinned on one person unless someone's clearly and individually responsible

If you're a Lead in Training: you're scored on Attendance and Damages only — no driver score yet, since LITs aren't driving the truck.

Where to check your own performance

Your general performance metrics come from the Einstein Games app — that's the same place you can already check your own numbers today, before a dedicated promotion-status view exists.

You get checked every month, automatically

Don't qualify this month? You don't have to do anything — you'll be looked at again next month. The hours you need are specific to the rung you're eligible to move into (check the ladder table for your number). On the standard track, your most recent ~3 months matter most, so a rough patch a while back doesn't follow you forever. On the fast track, it's your most recent ~4 months — one month longer, since fast track is asking for a longer stretch of excellent performance, not just a good recent one.

Lead in Training → Part-Time Lead is a little different

You only move into a Part-Time Lead seat once there's a business need for another Lead — but you still have to have the hours and performance requirement to move into that position (455 hrs, 7.0 bar, same as clearing LIT). Hours and performance get you eligible; the seat opening depends on business need. Ask your manager where that stands.

Part-Time Lead → Junior Lead is also a little different

A separate, later step. Getting from Part-Time Lead to Junior Lead isn't just about your hours — it clears at 400 hours worked as a lead, OR leading 80%+ of jobs across 2 back-to-back pay periods — and it's also based on business need for a Lead. Ask your manager where that stands. Note: hours worked as a Part-Time Lead don't carry forward into your Junior Lead hours count toward Senior Lead — that clock starts fresh once you're Junior Lead.

When your new pay starts

A promotion always takes effect at the start of the next pay period — not partway through one. Your title and your new pay change on the same day.

No grandfathering — but nobody loses anything either

Everyone gets evaluated under the new rules the moment this launches — including someone who was about to hit an old tenure-only promotion. Nobody loses their current title or pay; this only changes what earns the next one. Where you are today doesn't change.
